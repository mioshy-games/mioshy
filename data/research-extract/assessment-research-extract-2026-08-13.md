## 4. Thin, odd, or worth knowing before you quote it

Nothing here is interpreted. These are the places where a number is thinner, or means something
slightly different, than it looks.

### a. The brief says 8 changed questions. The versions table holds 7 slugs.

Seven slugs carry a version boundary. The count reconciles to 8 if you count *changes* rather than
*questions*: `q01_knowledge_world` changed meaning twice, so there are 8 meaning-changes across 7 slugs.
Six are stem rewrites; the seventh, `q20_biggest_gap`, kept its stem and rewrote an **option label** —
the option `knowing` went from *"להרגיש שמכירים אחד את השני באמת"* to *"להרגיש יותר תשוקה ומשיכה"*,
and its score moved from `love_map` to `passion_anticipation`. For an option-distribution table that is
exactly as disqualifying as a stem change, so it gets the era rule too.

### b. One question's dominant era is the OLD wording, not today's

`q19_rituals` — the dominant era is **v1** (n=687, the "fixed moments that belong only to us" wording),
and the current live wording (v2, n=43, about disclosing private thoughts without fear of judgement) is
the one dropped. The Hebrew published for q19 in this file is therefore **not** what the site asks today.
That is the rule working as written; it is only worth knowing before the text is quoted anywhere.

### c. Two questions carry a wording change the versions table does not treat as a meaning change

`journey_question_versions` records only changes judged to change the question's *meaning*. The full edit
log in `journey_questions_history` contains more rewrites than that. Most are cosmetic — first person to
second person, singular to plural — and most have a tail of 1 answer. Two do not, and both are large and
both appear in the published cross-tabs:
